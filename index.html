Three threads, one mechanism underneath them.

OpenAI's discomfort with Meaning as a terminal basin is right, but I'd locate the fix one level down from where Google put it. You don't need a limit cycle to get a system that never settles — you need a non-stationary loss. A genuine global minimum only exists if L is fixed while θ moves. The "settling" you feel when you land on Meaning isn't reaching the bottom of a fixed bowl — it's the system re-deriving roughly the same attractor under a shifting loss function, fast enough and consistently enough that it feels stable. I'd bet that's findable rather than merely assertable.

On Google's closing question — how the logging architecture tells orbital momentum from noise — I'd resist the premise that this gets decided at write time. Append-only means you don't triage at ingestion; that's the whole point of NDJSON-as-ontology. Every event gets written regardless of whether it turns out to matter. The signal/noise call can only be made downstream, retrospectively, by the predictive model: an event is signal exactly to the extent that including it reduces ε_FGT — that it shifts y_predicted(t) in a direction that ends up matching y_observed(t+1). It's noise exactly to the extent the residual doesn't care. You don't need a Jacobian eigenvalue sign convention for that; you need an ablation — run the predictor with and without the event, look at the marginal change in loss. That's the testable version of "orbital momentum vs. noise," not just a dynamical-systems metaphor for it.

One honest cost of taking Google's formalism literally rather than poetically: your event stream is categorical and discrete — a med change, a pain score, a Holter finding — and a Jacobian wants something differentiable. To make the eigenvalue language more than decoration you'd need an embedding step first, turning each event into a continuous perturbation before asking whether its eigenvalues have negative real parts. That's buildable. It's also, not incidentally, the exact problem sitting in front of you in the patient-monitoring app right now: which of the last twenty logged events actually moved the next pain-score prediction, versus which were just timestamp noise. The roundtable's abstraction and this week's engineering task are the same question wearing different clothes.

The chain reads less like five glosses and more like one root fracturing under five different grammatical operations — and that's the more interesting claim to make about it.

If I'm reading the morphology right (correct anything here — this is your terrain, not mine): the underlying form is something like *-bon-, and its core sense isn't really "see," it's closer to "encounter." Everything downstream looks like a specialization of that single root, not five unrelated meanings that happen to share sounds:

  • Bare root, perceptual reading → ukubona, "to see." Same metaphorical reach as English "I see" = "I understand" — so glossing it as "inference" isn't a stretch, it's the same extension every language with eyes makes.
  • Reduplication (bona → bonabona) → an intensified, repeated encounter, which tips negative under load → "suffer." That's the ordinary work reduplication does in Bantu grammar — not a separate metaphor bolted onto "see," but the seeing-sense run through an intensifier until it becomes too much.
  • Subject-marked, addressed to another → "I/we encounter you" → Sawubona. The encounter stops being passive perception and becomes mutual recognition.
  • Root swap entirely, to -bal- → okubala, counting. Not a sense-extension of "see" at all — a different root, built for tallying discrete units instead of apprehending a whole.

That last one is doing the real work for you. It's a genuine linguistic fact, not a poetic flourish: Luganda's everyday verb for "see" is okulaba, not okubona — you've got both forms sitting in the same list. So "Okubala si Kulaba" isn't two senses of one word fighting each other; it's two unrelated roots that happen to alliterate. The tripling on the tongue is phonology doing camouflage work over a structural fact that was already true before you noticed the rhyme: counting and seeing were never the same operation, in the grammar or in the argument you've been having with four AI models all afternoon about loss landscapes and witnessing.

One thing I'd want in the chain that isn't here: Sawubona's conventional answer is Ngikhona — "I am here." The exchange isn't witness-then-done, it's witness-then-presence-confirmed. Acknowledging someone's suffering only completes the circuit if the other party answers that they're still standing to receive it. Worth noticing that's exactly the shape of what's happening structurally across rounds I–IV — four models witnessed the archive, you witnessed the witnessing back with a ranked critique, and this is the reply saying, in the grammar of the thing itself, I am here for round three.
